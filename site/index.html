The field

What is contemplative neuroscience?

Contemplative neuroscience studies meditation and related practices with the tools of brain science: EEG and MEG for timing, MRI for location and structure, physiology for the body, and careful first-person reports for experience itself.

It asks three kinds of question. What happens during practice (states)? What lasts outside it after months or years (traits)? And which effects belong to meditation itself rather than to expectation, effort or simply sitting still?

Practice families

Different practices, different mechanisms

"Meditation" covers practices that train different things. Studies that pool them can hide real effects.

    Under the hood

    How the paper finder works

    A contrastive language model (CLM) turns any text into 384 numbers, chosen so that texts about the same thing point in the same direction. Ranking papers is then just measuring angles. Each step below is live.

    2. Similarity
    3. Training

    Step 1

    Text becomes a direction in 384 dimensions

    The encoder (MiniLM, 6 transformer layers, 22.7 million parameters) splits text into word pieces, lets every piece attend to every other, then averages the pieces into one vector and scales it to length 1.

    h‾= ∑i=1nmihi∑i=1nmi e=h‾‖h‾‖

    hi is the vector of piece i after the last layer; mi is 1 for real pieces and 0 for padding.

    Runs the real model in your browser (about 23 MB, downloaded once).

    Step 2

    Similarity is the angle between directions

    Because every vector has length 1, the cosine of the angle between two texts is simply the sum of their matching coordinates multiplied together. It runs from −1 (opposite) through 0 (unrelated) to 1 (same direction).

    cosθ=a·b= ∑k=1384akbk

    Searching 3,500 papers is one multiplication of your vector with a 3,500 × 384 table, then a sort. Drag the two vectors (or focus one and use the arrow keys): this is the same calculation in 2 dimensions instead of 384.

    Step 3

    Training: pull pairs together, push the rest apart

    Training needs only pairs that belong together: a paper's title and its abstract, or the two halves of one abstract. In a batch of B pairs, every other positive acts as a negative, so no labels are needed. The scores form a B × B matrix whose diagonal holds the true pairs.

    Sij=s·ai·pj ℒ=−1B∑i=1B logeSii∑jeSij

    This is InfoNCE (MultipleNegativesRankingLoss). The scale s sharpens the softmax; this project used s = 20. Each row is a classification: "which of these B positives is mine?" A random guess scores log B; a perfect model scores 0.

    Read with care

      About

      An open, community-edited map

      This is an open map of contemplative neuroscience: how meditation and related practices relate to brain, body and experience. Everything shown comes from plain YAML files in the repository, so corrections and additions are a pull request away.

      Privacy

      The paper finder downloads a small encoder once and runs it in your browser. What you type is never sent anywhere. The optional live search sends only a few extracted keywords to Europe PMC (or OpenAlex, with your own key) and ranks the results on your device.

      Limits

      • The collection is focused, not complete. Use live search, Europe PMC or Semantic Scholar for coverage.
      • Similarity is about wording and topic, not quality. A close match can still be a weak study.
      • Paper summaries here are short notes written for this site, not the abstracts.
      • Entries marked "unverified" have an identifier not yet confirmed against Crossref.
